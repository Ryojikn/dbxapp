# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solutions Architects sharing a live screen-share or async link with technical buyers: platform engineers, data engineering directors, and architects at companies evaluating the Databricks Lakehouse. The audience is sophisticated and spends their day in tools like VS Code, Linear, and dbt — they will notice sloppy UI immediately and lose trust in the underlying platform narrative.

## Product Purpose

A reference architecture demo app for the Databricks Lakehouse Platform. Shows a complete Bronze/Silver/Gold medallion pipeline with Unity Catalog governance, real-time ingestion via Auto Loader and Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines, live KPI dashboards with anomaly detection, and a natural-language chat interface over retail analytics data. Success means the audience finishes the demo believing Databricks is production-grade and worth evaluating seriously.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, capable, direct. Not a marketing site — a real tool. Demonstrates competence through design precision, not visual noise.

## Anti-references

- Databricks.com (marketing gloss, gradient heroes, large lifestyle photography)
- Generic SaaS dashboard templates (hero-metric cards, identical card grids, accent gradients)

## Design Principles

1. **Precision signals trust.** Every measurement, spacing decision, and typographic choice either builds or erodes the audience's confidence. Sloppy spacing says "prototype."
2. **Color carries meaning, not decoration.** Red is for Databricks identity and critical state. Green is success. Gray is structure. Never use color to make something feel "finished."
3. **Density earns respect.** The audience can read dense tables and multi-panel layouts. Don't pad things out to look "clean" — that reads as empty.
4. **Architecture is the hero.** The diagram and data flow should be effortlessly readable. Every visual choice should help the audience see the system, not the interface.
5. **The tool should disappear.** Navigation, tabs, forms, and controls should feel instantly familiar so the audience focuses on the data story, not on learning the UI.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High contrast ratios on all data labels and chart text. No information conveyed by color alone (architecture node shapes should differentiate by color + label). Reduce motion preference respected.
